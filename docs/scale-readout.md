# Timbangan: baca angka dari layar (scale readout)

When a food photo shows a kitchen scale's display, a second, tiny vision call reads the digits and those grams replace the model's guess on the item that was weighed. The scan shows **"Terbaca dari timbangan: 100 g"** under the row, with the photo estimate one tap away ("Perkiraan foto: 150 g"). Why: grams-from-photo is the weak point of the scan (`docs/ai-accuracy-log.md`, 2026-10-20: rice guessed at ~150 g where 100 g was eaten); a display is the one place the photo carries the exact number.

## Flag

`SCALE_READOUT_USER_IDS`: comma-separated user ids, or `*` for everyone (a Worker var in `apps/server/wrangler.jsonc`, like `DATASET_USER_IDS`). **On for everyone since 2026-10-24.** Before that it ran for the owner only: over 36 owner scans it read 7 displays, all 7 real scale photos (258-668 g, confidence 0.9-1), and gave no reading on the 29 photos without a scale. The cost, about 4.5 neurons per scan (about $0.00005 at $0.011 per 1,000 neurons), was judged acceptable. Unset or empty: no second call is made and `ai.analyzeFood` / `analyzeFoodStream` / `analyzeFoodRetry` return exactly what they returned before (no `scaleRead` key on any item). To switch it off, set the var to the owner's id (or empty) and deploy.

The camera tip (`dishware.scanHint`): `show` = a card is read for this user (`DISHWARE_USER_IDS`; builds before 1.1.0 only read `show` and say "card or scale"), `scale` = the scale display is read; 1.1.0 and the web show "punya timbangan dapur?" to scale-only users. The row's "Termasuk wadah?" takes the empty bowl or plate off a reading (`tareScaleRows`, `lib/scale-chip.ts`), and Koreksi has a `wadah` op for "668 itu termasuk mangkok".

## How it works (`packages/api/src/lib/scale-reading.ts`)

1. **A separate call, beside the scan** (`readScaleDisplay`): same model, same photo, `SCALE_PROMPT`, temperature 0, answers `{"scale":null}` (about 6 tokens) or `{"scale":{"reading","unit","confidence","appliesTo"}}`. It starts together with the scan and is awaited after it, so latency is the scan's; a failure or a 12 s timeout is a scan with no reading, never a failed scan. One retry on an unparseable answer.
2. **The model only transcribes.** `reading` is the digits as shown ("100", "3.5", "1,25"); `unit` is g, kg, oz, lb, ml or fl oz; code does the rest: either decimal mark, units to grams (oz 28.3495, lb 453.592, kg 1000, ml read 1:1 like the drink rule), a grams display with three decimals ("1.250", thousands or decimal?) rejected as ambiguous.
2b. **The container.** The same answer says what stands on the scale beside the food (`container`: bowl, plate, box, cup, bag, wrapper or none), its `material` (ceramic, plastic, paper, other) and whether the display was zeroed with it on (`tared`: true only with a TARE / NET / T mark on the display or a container that was clearly zeroed; unsure = false). A reading that includes a ceramic soup bowl is not soup: the owner's two soto scans saved 668 g (bowl inside) as the soup. When the model reports a container and `tared` is false, `applyScaleReading` takes the container's estimated weight off the reading before it is shared over the items (`CONTAINER_GRAMS`, the light end of what such dishware weighs, e.g. ceramic bowl 250 g, ceramic plate 300 g, paper box 40 g, bag or wrapper 5 g) and the chip says so, never silently: **"Terbaca dari timbangan: 668 g, dikurangi mangkuk ~250 g"**, with "Wadah ~-250 g" on the tare chip. The person can replace the estimate with a weighed container (the existing "Termasuk wadah?" panel; `tare 0` = "Tanpa wadah" puts the whole reading back). Nothing is taken off when: no container or `tared: true` or `tared` missing (`skipped` on the ledger), or the reading minus the container would leave under 20 g or under a fifth of the reading (`too_light`: the display can only have been zeroed). Registered plates (`dishware`) have no weight column and remembered tares live on the device only, so the estimate table is the only server-side source; the gates in 3 are unchanged and run on the raw reading.
3. **Gates** (`applyScaleReading`), a reading is set aside, never half-used, when: confidence < 0.75 (`SCALE_MIN_CONFIDENCE`); the display is 0 / 0.0 (tare, empty); digits do not parse; grams fall outside 1-5000; it names no item (several items) or only items without a weight; the total is more than 10 times away from the model's own total (`SCALE_MAX_RATIO`: a missed decimal point, not a weight).
4. **Where the grams land.** `appliesTo` = an item name (or several, `|`-separated): that item gets the grams (several: shared in the model's proportions, adding up exactly). `plate` (or no name): every food item shares the total in the model's proportions; drinks only when nothing else is there. A name that matches nothing covers the photo's only item, never one of several. Hidden cups are never covered. The grams shared are the reading minus the container (2b).
5. **Numbers follow** through `item-grams.ts` (shared with "Porsi kebiasaanmu"): kcal and macros scale with grams, then calibration (library or TKPI band scaled to the grams, density cap). A scale-read item skips the portion prior.

## Response and clients

An item with a reading carries `scaleRead: { active: "scale" | "ai", other: {grams, kcal, ...}, text, unit, grams, count, tare?, tareKind? }` (`tare` = grams of container taken off the reading, `tareKind` set while that is the scan's own estimate); the item's own numbers are the `active` side (`grams` = the whole reading in grams, `count` = rows it was shared over). `scale-chip.ts` (`swapScaleRead`, `scaleValue`, `scaleGrams`, `withoutScaleRead`) is shared by `apps/web` (`components/scan/scale-read-chips.tsx`) and `apps/native` (same name): one chip "Terbaca dari timbangan: 100 g" (or "3.5 oz (99 g)", or "..., dibagi ke 3 item") and one "Perkiraan foto: 150 g"; tapping the other puts its numbers on the row. Any other grams fix goes through the correction box as before. "Setengah porsi" drops the chip.

The ledger row (`ai_usage.usage_json`) keeps `aiItems` as the user saw them and gains `scale`: the reading, what became of it (`applied` item indexes, `before` = the model's own grams for them, or `skipped` + reason) the call's cost (`ms`, `usage`, `error`) and `container` {kind, material, tared, tare, skipped?} (what the model saw and the grams taken off, 0 with the reason in `skipped`).

**Removed rows.** The reading weighs everything on the scale, so rows the person drops hand their share back. Native: the checkmark on a row re-splits the reading over the rows that stay (`resplitScaleRows`, from the photo-estimate proportions, so putting a row back restores the first split). Web: a Koreksi that removes rows re-splits over the remaining rows the reading covered (`resplitScaleAfterCorrection`; grams the person stated in the same correction, and added rows, are held fixed and come off first). Not re-split when a covered row shows the photo estimate (the person picked that side).

**Where the grams came from.** Every item in `aiItems` / `retryItems` carries `gs`: `model` (photo estimate), `scale` (display, container already off), `prior` ("Porsi kebiasaanmu" started the row) or `user`. `savedItems` carry it too: an item keeps the source of the answer's item it matches (same name, or same grams under another name) when its grams are unchanged, anything else is `user` (typed, a chip, a correction, a container weight). Reports can split saved-vs-answer grams error by source from `usage_json` alone. `items_json` of a saved row carries the displayed list without the chip.

## Why not a rule inside the scan prompt

Tried first (the scale rule appended to the scan prompt, `scaleReading` after `items`). It read the digits right, but on the egg-on-a-scale photo the model dropped the keys of its item list in 8 of 15 runs (`"name":"telur ayam","makanan","satu butir","61",...`: unparseable, 0 of 15 for the baseline prompt on the same photo; the harness replays the identical retry request from its cache, production would re-sample). A second call leaves the scan's own request and answer untouched. It costs about 507 prompt tokens and 6 completion tokens when there is no display (about 4.5 neurons against 13 for the scan, measured on 101 calls), so before this goes beyond the allowlist decide whether a 35% higher scan cost is acceptable or gate the call.

## Eval

`bun run scripts/eval-scans.ts --scale-photos --variant baseline --variant scale-readout --reps 2`. The `scale-readout` variant runs the baseline scan request (its answer is replayed, so the comparison is paired exactly) plus the scale call, then `applyScaleReading`. The report's "Scale readout" section counts false reads (a reading on a photo with no display), digits exact and the grams error before and after on photos with a display. A photo shows a display when `scale.json` sits beside it (`{"reading":"100","unit":"g","appliesTo":"plate"}` or `"appliesTo":["nasi putih"]`, written by hand after looking at the photo; under `.tmp/dataset/...`, never committed); a photo without the file is taken to show none. `--scale-photos` adds three public Wikimedia Commons photos of a scale display (flour bag on a Scaleman, egg on a glass scale, tomato on a B05 scale; the display is the only truth) because no dataset photo has a display; they are downloaded to `.tmp/eval/scale` and run only for the read-accuracy and gram checks. Numbers in `docs/ai-accuracy-log.md`.

`scale.json` also takes `"container"` (bowl, plate, box, cup, bag, wrapper, none: what stands on the scale), `"tared"` (true when the display was zeroed with it on) and `"foodGrams"` (the food's own weight, when the container was weighed). With `container` the report adds the container the model read and the grams taken off per run, and counts kind right / tared flag right / taken off when it should be / taken off when it should not. A photo whose display includes a container has no food truth without `foodGrams`: its error columns then show the distance from the display, not an error.

**2026-10-27, container-aware readout** (`scale-readout`, 2 reps, 11 labelled display photos: 8 of the owner's, the 3 `--scale-photos`; 18 owner photos without a display; the owner photos are labelled by hand and git-ignored). Before (readout without container): digits exact 19 of 20 (one `260.`, the LCD's trailing point, was read as unreadable and set aside; fixed in `parseScaleDigits`), 0 of 14 container photos had the container accounted for, 0 false reads in 36 runs on photos without a display. After: digits exact 20 of 20, container kind read right 14 of 14, tared flag right 14 of 14 (all 14 were untared; no tared display photo exists yet, so a true `tared` is only covered by unit tests), container taken off on 14 of 14 and wrongly on 0 of 0, 0 false reads in 36. The two soto-in-a-ceramic-bowl photos: 668 g became 418 g (bowl estimated 250 g); the bowl was never weighed, so there is no food truth and the true error is unknown (a ceramic soup bowl weighs 250-450 g, so the old 668 g overstated the soup by 60-206% and the new 418 g by 0-92%). Plate in a cardboard box 270 g -> 230 g food (box 40 g), plate on paper 313 g -> 308 g, fruit in a plastic bag 256-260 g -> 251-255 g, a flour bag 243 g -> 238 g, egg and tomato on the glass scale (nothing under them) untouched.
